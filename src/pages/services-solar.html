There are two ways to buy solar in Zimbabwe. You can buy a package — a fixed number of panels, a fixed inverter, a fixed battery — and hope it covers your life. Or you can start with what you actually run, for how many hours, and work backwards to the equipment that carries it. We only do the second one, because the first is how people end up disappointed with a system that cost real money.

How we size a system

Before quoting we want to know what is on your essential list and what is negotiable. A fridge, lights, Wi-Fi, TV and phone charging is a modest system. Add a borehole pump, a geyser, a microwave and air conditioning and you are in a different class of installation entirely — not because the panels change much, but because the inverter has to survive the surge and the battery has to hold the evening.

  • Load audit — every appliance you need to keep running, its draw and its hours
  • Autonomy — how many hours or days you want to ride through without sun or grid
  • Surge — motor starting loads from pumps, compressors and power tools
  • Roof — orientation, pitch, shading and structural capacity
  • Budget — and where a system can be expanded later rather than replaced

The honest version: if your budget will not carry your whole load, we will tell you which loads to move off the system rather than quietly undersizing it and letting you find out in the first cloudy week.

On-grid, hybrid and off-grid

Grid-tied (on-grid)

Solar feeds your building alongside the ZESA supply and reduces what you draw from the meter. It is the cheapest way to cut a bill because there is no battery, but on its own it gives you nothing during an outage.

Hybrid

The configuration most Harare properties actually want. Solar and battery work with the grid: sun charges the battery and runs the load, the grid tops up when needed, and when the supply drops the changeover happens without you noticing. Lights do not flicker, computers do not reboot.

Off-grid

For farms, lodges, clinics, boreholes and properties where the connection is unreliable or does not exist. Everything comes from panels and storage, so sizing has to be right — there is no grid to hide a mistake.

Solar borehole pumping

One of the highest-value solar applications in Zimbabwe. A solar array drives the borehole pump directly during daylight and fills elevated storage, and gravity does the rest — no battery required, no fuel, and water available whether or not the grid is up. We size the array to the pump and the tank to your daily demand. See also boreholes and water systems.

Solar geysers

Water heating is usually the single biggest electrical load in a home. Moving it to an evacuated-tube or flat-plate solar geyser takes that load off both your bill and your battery — which often means a smaller, cheaper inverter system does the same job.

Why installations fail early

Panels rarely fail. What fails is everything around them — and almost all of it is avoidable:

  • Undersized or unprotected DC cable, and no surge protection on the array
  • Batteries discharged deeper and more often than their specification allows
  • An inverter sized on running watts with no allowance for pump and motor surge
  • Poor earthing, so the first serious storm takes out the electronics
  • Everything wired to the system instead of a properly separated essential-load board

We fit the protection as standard rather than as an optional extra, because a burnt inverter in year two costs everyone more than the isolator that would have prevented it.

Maintenance and expansion

Systems need occasional attention: panels washed, connections checked and torqued, battery state reviewed, firmware and settings adjusted as your consumption changes. We service what we install, and we will happily assess a system somebody else installed and tell you where it stands.

Solar FAQs

What people ask before going solar

How much does a solar system cost?

It depends entirely on the load and the autonomy you want. A backup system for lights, Wi-Fi, TV and a fridge is a fraction of the cost of one that runs a borehole pump, geyser and air conditioning. We size from your actual consumption and quote itemised equipment and labour, so you can see exactly what you are paying for.

Will solar run my geyser and borehole pump?

It can, but both are heavy loads and there is usually a smarter way. A solar geyser heats water directly and takes that load off the system entirely. A borehole is often best served by a dedicated solar pumping array filling elevated storage during the day. Doing it that way is normally cheaper than buying enough inverter and battery to brute-force both.

Lithium or lead-acid batteries?

Lithium (LiFePO₄) for almost every application. You can use far more of the rated capacity, the cycle life is several times longer, it needs no maintenance and it can be expanded in modules. Lead-acid looks cheaper on the quote and rarely is once you count replacements.

Can I start small and add to it later?

Yes, if it is planned that way from the start. We size the inverter and the wiring for where you intend to end up, then let you add panels and battery modules as budget allows. Retrofitting expansion onto a system that was not designed for it usually means replacing the inverter.

Do you service systems installed by someone else?

Yes. We will assess an existing installation, tell you honestly what is sound and what is not, and quote on repairs, protection upgrades or expansion.
