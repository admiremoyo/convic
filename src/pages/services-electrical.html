Electrical work is the part of a building nobody sees and everybody depends on. Done well it is invisible for decades. Done badly it shows up as tripping boards, scorched terminals, burnt-out motors and, at worst, a fire. Covcro installs and maintains electrical systems across Harare for homeowners, developers, businesses, farms and industrial clients.

New electrical installations

We prefer to be on site before the concrete is. On a new build the electrical first fix — conduit, draw boxes, sleeves and earthing set into the slab and walls before they close up — determines how tidy and how serviceable the finished installation will be. Get it wrong and the alternative is chasing finished walls and breaking new floors.

From there we take the installation through to completion: distribution boards, circuits, lighting, sockets, geyser and appliance points, outside lighting, gate motors and standby changeover, all tested and labelled before handover.

  • First-fix conduit, sleeves and slab work coordinated with the builder's programme
  • Distribution board design, supply and installation
  • Lighting design — recessed downlights, concealed LED, decorative and feature fittings
  • Power circuits, dedicated appliance points and geyser wiring
  • Earthing and bonding, surge protection and RCD/earth-leakage protection
  • Standby and inverter changeover, essential-load boards
  • Gate motors, electric fencing supply, external and security lighting

Rewiring and upgrades

Older Harare properties were wired for a fraction of the load a modern household puts on them. Add a borehole pump, two geysers, air conditioning and an inverter to a board designed in the 1980s and something has to give.

We rewire and upgrade in stages where a property is occupied, so you are not without power for days at a time. Where a full rewire is not warranted, we will say so — often the honest fix is a new board, a proper earth and two or three new dedicated circuits rather than stripping the whole house.

Commercial and industrial installations

Shops, offices, warehouses, workshops, clinics and factories have obligations a house does not: continuity of supply, emergency lighting, three-phase distribution and equipment that is expensive to leave idle.

  • Three-phase distribution, sub-mains and reticulation
  • Motor control centres, starters, contactors and control panels
  • Machine and plant connections, isolators and interlocks
  • Warehouse and factory high-bay lighting
  • Emergency lighting and essential-services boards
  • Power factor correction and metering
  • Planned preventive maintenance contracts and shutdown work

Working around production. Industrial clients rarely have the luxury of a week's downtime. We plan the outages that a job genuinely needs, agree them with you in advance, and do the rest live-adjacent so your plant keeps running.

11kV and medium-voltage lines

Not every electrical contractor works above 400V. We install and maintain 11kV and medium-voltage overhead lines, transformer connections and site reticulation — the infrastructure that gets bulk supply from the network to a farm, a mine, an industrial stand or a new development.

  • 11kV and MV overhead line construction and extensions
  • Pole erection, stringing, stays and hardware
  • Transformer plinths, mounting and connection
  • Ring-main units, switchgear and MV isolation
  • Site reticulation from the transformer to distribution points
  • Line inspection, fault repair and maintenance

Medium-voltage work is unforgiving and is planned accordingly — permits and outages arranged with the utility, the line proved dead and earthed before anyone touches it, and the work signed off before it is re-energised.

Fault finding and maintenance

A breaker that trips "for no reason" always has a reason. Finding it takes method and instruments, not guesswork and replacement parts. We trace earth leakage, nuisance tripping, overheating joints, failed neutrals, voltage drop on long runs and the damage that a bad supply does to motors and electronics.

  • Earth-leakage and nuisance-tripping diagnosis
  • Overload and voltage-drop investigation
  • Board thermal inspection and re-termination
  • Storm and surge damage repair
  • Scheduled maintenance for landlords, body corporates and businesses

Why bring the electrical and the plumbing to one contractor

On any build, the two trades that most often collide are electrical and plumbing — conduit and pipework competing for the same slab, the same chase and the same ceiling void. When they are two different companies, the collision becomes a negotiation, and the client pays for the delay.

Electrical FAQs

Common electrical questions

Do you work on new builds as well as existing properties?

Both. On new builds we prefer to be appointed before the slab so the first-fix conduit and sleeves go in with the reinforcement. On existing properties we handle rewires, board upgrades, additional circuits and fault repairs, and we stage the work so an occupied house is not without power for days.

My board keeps tripping — is that a big job?

Usually not, but it does need diagnosing rather than guessing. Repeated tripping is almost always earth leakage from one appliance or circuit, a damaged cable, or moisture ingress. We isolate the fault with instruments and tell you what it will cost before we start replacing anything.

Can you install three-phase power for machinery?

Yes. We install three-phase distribution, sub-mains, isolators, starters and motor control centres for workshops, factories and farms, and we handle the connection of the plant itself.

Do you do 11kV work, or only low voltage?

We work on 11kV and medium-voltage overhead lines, transformer connections, switchgear and site reticulation, as well as everything on the low-voltage side. That includes new line construction and extensions for farms, mines, industrial stands and developments.

Will you coordinate with my builder and other trades?

Yes, and it makes a real difference to the finish. We work to the builder's programme, get the first fix in before the pour and coordinate with plumbing so conduit and pipework are not fighting for the same route. Where we are doing the plumbing too, that coordination is automatic.
